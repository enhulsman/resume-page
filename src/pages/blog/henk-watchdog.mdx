On [his own page](https://henk.hulsman.dev), Henk sits by the window. He's painted in Delft
blue, an old man in a flat cap on a kitchen chair, coffee in hand, clock on the wall, keeping
an eye on the street. When something happens out there, he tells you. When nothing happens,
he says nothing at all, which from across the street looks exactly like a man who's nodded off.

The real Henk runs on a Raspberry Pi 5 in my homelab. He listens to the alerts my monitoring
sends, works out which ones matter, and messages me on Signal when one does, with what broke,
what he already checked and how to pick it up. Most weeks that's one or two messages, plus
the odd reminder I asked him for. I don't talk to him much otherwise, which is pretty much
the point.

Most of my homelab got built that way: long sessions with Claude, everything checked properly,
and then I stop looking at it. I'd argue that's the only sane way to run a homelab on evenings
and weekends, you put the effort in once so you don't have to babysit it after. It does mean a
quiet system has to be able to show it's alive, because I'm not going to notice when it isn't.

A watcher who says nothing is the hardest case of all.

For Henk, nodding off has a specific shape. He listens to my ntfy server over one long-lived connection,
and if the network path dies without either end hearing about it, his side just keeps waiting,
politely, for a message that's never coming. No error, no log line, nothing. That's the problem
I promised to write about at the end of [the last Henk post](/blog/henk-exactly-once).

The answer was already on the wire. ntfy sends a small keepalive frame down the connection every
45 seconds, whether anything is happening or not, so Henk now treats those as a pulse. If 135
seconds pass without one, three missed beats, he drops the connection and reconnects from the
last message he saw, so nothing gets lost on the way.

## Freezing the network

On 6 August I tested it the mean way. Henk reaches the outside world through a small tailscale
container next to him, and I froze that container for 170 seconds. Freezing is nastier than
stopping it, nothing gets closed, the connection just goes quiet, which is exactly the case
the pulse is for.

The last keepalive arrived at 13:07:46. At 13:10:01, 135 seconds later to the second, Henk gave
up on the connection and started reconnecting, waiting a little longer between tries while the
network stayed frozen. A bit over a minute later he was back, picking up from the same message
as before.

## 80 an hour

Once an hour he writes one line to his log:

```
intake liveness: still delivering; 80 proof-of-life frames in the last 3600s;
last proof-of-life 2026-10-09T07:24:40 (0s ago), last reconnect never, penalty 0
```

80 is the number that matters. 80 times 45 seconds is an hour, so 80 means every beat arrived
and the street was quiet. His current container has written 282 of these lines since
27 September, and 280 of them say 80. The other two say 82, both hours when something did
happen: rp2's node_exporter falling over for the third time in a day and a half, and a DNS
check on my VPS that blipped for about a minute. "Last reconnect never" on every one of them,
so one unbroken connection for eleven days.

I don't read that line day to day, and I don't need to, the reconnecting happens without me.
The pulse keeps Henk listening, it doesn't tell me anything. For the case where Henk himself is
gone, his container crashed or the Pi did, a Grafana rule now watches his three containers and
pings me on Discord within about ten minutes. It deliberately goes around him, he shouldn't be
the one triaging his own death. If the containers are up but he keeps failing to reconnect,
that still only shows in the log.

Most hours there's nothing to report, though. He writes 80 and goes back to his coffee.
